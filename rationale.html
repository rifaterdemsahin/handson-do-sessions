Why We DO: The Rationale

1. The Founder Must Keep Dancing

In community platforms like Skool, live workshops often face an awkward early barrier: people don't always show up. Schedules clash, time zones collide, or social anxiety prevents people from committing to an interactive group call.

Traditional hosts get discouraged, cancel calls, or postpone sessions. We do the exact opposite:

  • Locked Timelines: Monday, Tuesday, Friday are inviolable shoot blocks.
  • Zero Audience Dependency: The session happens regardless of whether zero people or one hundred people log on.
  • Live Co-Working Flow: If a member hops on, they can quietly watch over the shoulder and ask questions in the chat while the founder builds. If nobody logs on, the founder simply executes the build uninterrupted.

By treating every timeline as a live broadcast and recording session, there is zero wasted energy or awkward waiting rooms.

🛡️

2. Helping the Audience Left Behind in Fear

The rapid pace of AI has created an unspoken crisis among software developers, engineers, and creators: paralyzing fear.

People feel overwhelmed by the sheer velocity of autonomous agents, prompt techniques, and AI coding tools. They worry they are being replaced or permanently left behind, but they freeze because they don't know the exact first step to take.

  • No Pressure to Perform: Traditional workshops demand participation. Here, scared beginners can lurk silently and watch real work happen without fear of judgment.
  • Demystifying the Struggle: Viewers see that even seasoned engineers hit syntax bugs, rate limits, and compiler errors. Real engineering isn't magic prompt dust—it's iterative doing.
  • Action Replaces Anxiety: Watching an actual project from github.com/rifaterdemsahin go from blank terminal to working software gives members tangible steps they can copy and test themselves.
📚

3. The DO Sessions Pile Up (The Power of Compounding)

Every shoot on Monday, Tuesday, and Friday is recorded in high-bitrate MKV on the Mac M1, remuxed, and cataloged.

As week follows week, an undeniable asset emerges: the DO sessions pile up.

  • A Compounding Vault: A library of 10, 30, 50 hands-on code sessions covering beginner setups, tool routing, subagent pools, and RAG pipelines.
  • Asynchronous Lifeline: The member who was too busy or intimidated on Tuesday morning can watch the replay on Friday night at 1.5x speed.
  • Social Proof & Inevitability: When new members join the Skool community, they don't see empty promises or slide decks. They see dozens of hours of concrete, working code.
🚪

4. Lowering the Threshold: Come, Watch, and Build

Watching the founder work live creates psychological safety. It invites people into the arena without demanding that they fight on day one.

They can:

  • Drop in live on Skool: Lurk with a coffee, observe the screen share, or drop a live question in chat while the founder writes code.
  • Watch the recording: Pause, rewind, clone the GitHub repository, and follow along line-by-line.
  • Catch the YouTube segment: Absorb the high-level thought process in a crisp 10-minute edit.

Eventually, the observer becomes a doer. The spectator picks up the keyboard and starts building.
